# Release-candidate photography review — 22 October 2026

Ten real photographs downloaded from Wikimedia Commons: nine CC BY-SA (versions 3.0 or 4.0), one CC0. Each creator, source, licence and original dimensions was checked against the Commons file-page content. All ten local 1280-pixel derivatives were individually visually inspected. Originals are unchanged; only orientation-aware resizing and WebP conversion was applied. Layout cropping is declared for the consuming site. No generated scene, fabricated activity, borrowed Instagram photo or remote hotlink is included.

## Scope and files

- `assets/cleared/release-*.jpg`: ten stable-ID originals, approximately 49.7 MB total.
- `assets/optimized/release-*-640.webp` and `release-*-1280.webp`: twenty derivatives, approximately 2.34 MB total.
- `content/data/media.json`: rights, provenance, creator, source, licence, checksum, dimensions, subject, caption, crop guidance, visual-review status and derivative paths.
- `content/data/release-media-overrides.json`: candidate-only placement mapping. Shared temporary images are not globally replaced because their reuse across unrelated stories would misidentify sites.
- `sources/*.json`: concise factual source-page verification records. These are not API snapshots. Commons API access timed out; file-page content was read through the web reader and original binaries downloaded from the public Wikimedia upload server.
- `download-results.json`, `rights-review.json`, `place-image-attempt-manifest.json`: acquisition, rights and coverage evidence.
- `contact-sheet.jpg`: visual overview. Individual files, not just this sheet, were reviewed.

## Composition decisions

The Shah Jahan Mosque brick arcade is the principal portrait image for About/Why. The tiled courtyard is a clear supporting sacred-architecture image. Badshahi Mosque's overcast courtyard remains a colour-led homepage candidate; its night view is a final-CTA candidate with top-anchored cropping to retain the tall minaret finial. The source-processed monochrome Badshahi study is supporting editorial imagery, not a replacement for every colour image.

Takht-i-Bahi is a strong layered archaeological landscape. Rohtas's gateway should retain its crown and arch rather than be squeezed into a shallow banner. Shalamar's arches and basin illustrate the garden component, explicitly not Lahore Fort. The Moenjodaro and Baltit images are more documentary: their muted shadows and partial-facade composition mean they are not recommended as promotional heroes. Baltit and Rakaposhi should remain visible together.

## Coverage and honest limitations

The ten photos provide correctly identified alternatives for seven of the 606 canonical heritage places. The per-place manifest inventories current image fields and all seven regional DOAM catalogue image fields. 210 places have a catalogue/source-image candidate in those records; that does not establish permission. 599 places have no cleared alternative from this pass. The manifest does not claim that 606 individual web searches occurred.

No suitable, verified selection was procured for founder portraits, objects/material culture, living craft activity, Virsapur workshop/research/conservation teams, project achievements or event documentation. Those contexts should use the release's no-image treatment unless separately documented media is supplied. Do not use generic architecture as proof of Virsapur's own work.

The acquisition subtask left Preview source imagery and existing image files unchanged. The subsequent integration pass connected candidate-only placements and completed actual responsive page-crop checks, recorded in PAGE_CROP_REVIEW.json and the current screenshots. The mobile closing photograph now uses a75% horizontal,0% vertical focal point to retain its right-hand minaret. Licence attribution and BY-SA derivative licensing remain visible through the release's image-credits page. Source/Preview images do not acquire release permission through this review.
